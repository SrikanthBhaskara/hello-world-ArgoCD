# Senior Engineering Delivery and Leadership Deep Notes

## Why This Topic Matters
- At 5 to 7 years, interviewers expect ownership beyond feature coding.
- They want to hear how you review, document, mentor, estimate, and lead through incidents.

## Code Reviews
- focus on correctness, maintainability, security, and operational risk
- explain why a change is risky, not only that it is different from your preference

## ADRs
- Architecture Decision Records capture important decisions, options, tradeoffs, and consequences
- useful when systems evolve over time and many people contribute

## Estimation
- give ranges, assumptions, and risks
- separate known work from discovery work

## Mentoring
- unblock juniors
- explain design intent
- review with teaching mindset
- create reusable guidance, not only one-off answers

## Incident Command
- define roles clearly
- stabilize service first
- communicate facts, not guesses
- assign someone to timeline and coordination

## Postmortems
- focus on system learning, not blame
- document trigger, impact, timeline, root causes, and action items

## Stakeholder Communication
- translate technical risk into business impact
- explain tradeoffs simply
- report status clearly during delivery and incidents

## Interview Questions

### What changes from 5 years to 7 years?
Short answer:
More ownership, architecture thinking, mentoring, and incident leadership.

Better answer:
At 5 years a strong engineer is often a reliable independent contributor. By 7 years, interviewers expect you to drive technical decisions, review others effectively, handle ambiguity, mentor engineers, and lead operationally risky work with clear tradeoff thinking.

### What makes a good postmortem?
Short answer:
It teaches the team how to reduce repeat failure without blaming individuals.

Better answer:
A strong postmortem explains what happened, why defenses did not prevent it, how detection and response worked, and what concrete system changes will reduce recurrence. It turns an incident into organizational learning rather than just documentation.
